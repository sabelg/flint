# Flint animation and physics review

Three specialist AI reviewers examined character animation, real-time physics, and gameplay integration at commit `c72c926` on November 26, 2026. The recommended first milestone is a standalone, playable character that can walk onto a moving deck, stop, fall, and get back up. This gives animators a performance they can judge and engineers the same sequence to replay under test.

## Second review

**Pivot playback.** In the first motion-matching replay, the actor turned 90° but the animation rewound four times. The final pose came from frame 6 of the pivot. The graph played forward but switched actions before the final key, at 87.86°. The course now commits a requested quarter-turn to the authored Pivot clip through its landing. Locomotion matching resumes afterward. Both modes now turn 90.00002°, traverse frames 0–21 without rewinding and retain the right-foot contact for all 43 simulation ticks. The revised landing keeps 3 cm of pelvis flexion. The planted ankle moved 13.0 mm with the graph and 14.7 mm with the matcher. Reordering all six GLB clips produced identical per-tick traces and contact sheets. [Course playback](../examples/character_course/course.mojo) · [Capture checks](../examples/character_course/main.mojo)

**Physical recovery.** The original elbow swing limits clipped 58 of 204 sampled elbow targets in the supine get-up and 26 of 228 in the prone get-up. Calibrating the elbow hinges to the rig's flexion axis accommodated all those targets. Under the same 12 N·s sideways shove, motor-driven elbow error fell from 0.0661 to 0.0394 radians RMS. Whole-body joint position error stayed near 0.386 m, and physical recovery stayed at 0/3 on the original standing criterion. The final half-second explains what the old proximity metric missed. At least one foot was within 2 cm of the floor on every tick, while neither foot carried any of the 623 N mean floor load. The next recovery experiment should test whether a controller can shift load to the feet before raising the pelvis. [Rig](../examples/character_course/performer.mojo) · [Recovery experiment](../examples/character_course/recovery_bench.mojo)

**Editing a take.** Re-exporting an edited Walk clip changed its name and lost its footstep events. The take writer now preserves clip metadata, independent channel times, scale tracks and interpolation. Optimization keeps the incoming and outgoing tangents of synchronized cubic keys. A round-trip regression edits the bundled performer, appends the take, reloads it and compares the key data and events. [Take export regression](../tests/test_flint_v5_take_export.mojo) · [glTF animation sampler specification](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html#animation-sampler)

**Rotating colliders.** Fast-spinning boxes, capsules and convex hulls now sweep their rotation and translation against stationary primitive and convex colliders. In the rotating-bar case, first contact occurred at 0.39966 of a tick against a 2,049-pose reference of 0.39990. With 0.8 m of translation in the same tick, the fractions were 0.41242 and 0.41309. Both 3 mm clearance controls completed the tick. Pairs involving compound colliders, mesh or heightfield terrain, or a moving target retain the translation-only path. On reaching its work limit, the sweep can stop at the last verified safe pose. A confirmed contact within the collision tolerance is required to apply a collision impulse. [Sweep and response](../src/flint/physics/world.mojo) · [Geometry regressions](../tests/test_flint_v5_convex_terrain_ccd.mojo)

The second-round standalone gate passed on an isolated copy of the final sources with warnings treated as errors. All 505 end-to-end tests, five subsystem labs, six golden-frame comparisons and 33 performance checks passed. The run used the host’s higher-frequency CPU cores.

## First implementation and measured results

The [playable character course](../examples/character_course/README.md) implements the graph and motion-matching comparison on an original two-skin performer. Both modes run the same 465 ticks of input, including stepping onto the moving deck, stopping, pivoting, falling, and recovering. The course records final ankle positions and the deck transform, and checks foot drift during 80 consecutive ticks of planted stance. The first visual replay exposed a capsule-centered rig recovering below its supporting deck. The recovery state machine now takes the authored floor height into account.

| Course measurement | Graph | Motion matching |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ticks from walk input until forward travel exceeds 1 cm | 2 | 1 |
| Forward travel before stop input | 2.153 m | 2.175 m |
| Travel relative to the deck in the first 30 ticks after stop input | 0.249 m | 0.245 m |
| Largest displacement of either planted ankle during the 80-tick deck hold | 5.1 mm | 6.9 mm |

The first-round regression suites covered scene-loaded graph binding and footstep events, hot reload, multi-skin deformation, controller-resolved root motion at three render rates, recovery snapshots, full-shape translational CCD and convex terrain contact. The FLAN reader accepts legacy positional graphs. Older cooked archives need to be recooked to include the animated GLB source. [Asset and skin tests](../tests/test_flint_v5_scene_animation.mojo) · [Character integration](../tests/test_flint_v5_character_motion_integration.mojo) · [Collision tests](../tests/test_flint_v5_convex_terrain_ccd.mojo)

An independent [physical recovery benchmark](../examples/character_course/recovery_bench.mojo) compares joint motors and passive motion after the same lateral shove. Neither controller stood up in three runs per mode. The playable get-up uses authored recovery clips and inertial blending. A learned physical recovery controller remains a research target.

The complete standalone gate passed on an isolated copy of these sources, including 505 end-to-end tests, all five subsystem labs, six golden-frame comparisons and 33 performance checks. Two earlier runs exceeded existing timing limits in networking and rendering. Both binaries passed standalone reruns. The final end-to-end gate ran on the host's higher-frequency CPU cores with the original thresholds.

The findings below describe the audited commit and explain the changes.

## Findings that change the priorities

**1. The standard asset path loses the clip table.** The asset resolver loads a binary `.flanim` and registers it with an empty list. The animator requires a valid graph and at least one clip to be configured. A direct clip can still play, concealing the failed graph binding. The harness manually constructs and registers a table. Resolve each graph's ordered clip metadata against the actor's model, including authored events. Test two actors sharing a graph, a scene reload, and a missing clip that produces an actionable diagnostic. [Asset resolver](../src/flint/assets/asset_plugin.mojo#L691) · [Animator precondition](../src/flint/anim/animator.mojo#L238) · [Harness registration](../examples/test_harness_game/src/gameplay_core.mojo#L554)

**2. Attaching a ragdoll erased a planted foot correction.** In a headless EngineApp probe, a seven-node biped stood on a 25 cm step for 12 frames at 60 Hz. With an 8 cm ankle offset, the solver reported a locked target of 33.0 cm in both runs. The final ankle reached 32.99 cm without a ragdoll. Attaching a one-limb ragdoll in its ordinary animated state left the ankle at 8.0 cm, or 17 cm below the step surface. The animation plugin solves foot IK, then runs the ragdoll system. For an entity with a populated ragdoll, that system takes the animator's earlier pose. In the animated state it writes the pose back to the skeleton. Assign pose ownership by state so ordinary playback preserves the corrected skeleton, while an active ragdoll can drive the bones it owns. Check the final ankle and socket transforms after all systems have run. [System order](../src/flint/anim/plugin.mojo#L1398) · [Pose supplied to ragdoll](../src/flint/anim/plugin.mojo#L1249) · [Animated-state write](../src/flint/anim/flanim_ecs.mojo#L987)

**3. Character movement and foot placement need a shared account of contact.** Graph root motion edits the actor transform during the frame update. The fixed-step controller sweeps its desired velocity and can adopt an externally edited transform. Meanwhile, foot IK retains world X/Z during stance and only updates the height, even when the supporting surface moves sideways or turns. Pass intended root displacement through the controller, feed accepted displacement back to animation, and retain planted contacts in the supporting body's local coordinates. The wall and deck cases below are proposed integration tests. [Root motion](../src/flint/anim/plugin.mojo#L1072) · [Controller synchronization](../src/flint/physics/plugin.mojo#L2278) · [Controller sweep](../src/flint/physics/plugin.mojo#L2329) · [Stance lock](../src/flint/anim/procedural.mojo#L736)

**4. Continuous collision misses an off-center obstacle within a long body's sweep.** A standalone probe used a dynamic box with half-extents (0.10, 1.50, 0.15) m at the origin, moving along X at 180 m/s with CCD enabled and zero gravity. A static post with half-extents (0.05, 0.10, 0.15) m stood at X = 2 m. At 60 Hz the centered post produced a rebound (final X = 1.734 m, velocity = −18.0 m/s). Moving the post to Y = 1.20 m, still inside the box's vertical span, let the box cross at essentially full speed (X = 2.999 m, velocity = 179.94 m/s). Splitting the same interval into 64 steps produced a collision response (final X velocity = 104.69 m/s). The broad phase expands the center path by the smallest half-extent. Fix candidate coverage for the full translated shape and keep a separate test for rotation. [CCD candidate bounds and shape dispatch](../src/flint/physics/world.mojo#L4439)

**5. The existing assets leave important artistic judgments open.** The harness hero has three nodes, Root, Spine, and Hand.R. The lab's prone and supine get-ups both contain the same constant pelvis translation. Those fixtures can catch wiring errors. A legged character with distinct performances would reveal foot sliding, weight shifts, silhouette changes and recovery pops. The ECS skinning path also picks skin zero and the first weighted mesh, so a separate jacket on the same performer would test a real production asset case. [Harness asset](../examples/test_harness_game/assets/chars/hero.glb) · [Recovery clips](../labs/lab_anim.mojo#L89) · [ECS skinning](../src/flint/render/plugin.mojo#L1022)

## Recommended work order

The measurements below are proposed acceptance criteria. Build the fixtures in Flint's standalone Mojo repository with a redistributable rig and clips. Model weights can stay external.

| Order and owner | Deliverable | Acceptance scene |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Animation runtime + technical animator | Bind scene-loaded graphs and add one legged, skinned reference actor with idle, walk, stop and pivot clips. | From a clean scene load, switch idle → walk → stop via graph parameters. Verify clip identity, changing leg poses and exactly one event per authored foot strike. Repeat after reload using only the normal asset and animation plugins. |
| 2 · Gameplay physics + animation runtime | Give the controller authority over root displacement and preserve the final IK pose. | Walk into a wall, climb a step and ride a deck that translates 1 m and turns 30°. At a fixed 60 Hz simulation, replay at 30/60/120 render frames per second. Require the capsule to stay on the approach side of the wall, matching fixed-tick endpoints within 1 cm, and at most 2 cm of planted-foot drift in deck coordinates. Repeat with an animated ragdoll attached. |
| Parallel with 1–2 · Collision engineer | Correct long-body CCD and verify terrain contact geometry. | Turn the offset-post probe into a regression with a contact assertion and a centered control. For convex terrain contacts, place a wedge with a clear hull but overlapping bounds, then lower it to genuine contact against equivalent mesh and heightfield surfaces. Both representations should agree on separation and first touch within the configured contact tolerance. [Mesh fallback](../src/flint/physics/world.mojo#L4118) · [Heightfield fallback](../src/flint/physics/world.mojo#L4160) |
| 3 · Technical animator + physics | Add distinct face-up and face-down recoveries and preserve their blending state across restore. | After a fall, review hand placement, weight transfer and the first supported step at normal speed and frame by frame. Restore mid-get-up into a fresh app and compare every fixed-tick bone pose, contact and event with uninterrupted playback. The component saves the timer, while the blend uses additional history. [Snapshot fields](../src/flint/anim/plugin.mojo#L633) · [Recovery blend](../src/flint/anim/flanim_ecs.mojo#L1074) |
| 4 · Animation systems + animator | Put the existing motion matcher behind the same playable controls. | Reuse the stop and pivot course. Display desired trajectory, selected clip and frame, and stance contacts. Compare stop distance, response time, foot drift and visible transitions against the graph-driven version on identical input. [Feature baking and search](../src/flint/anim/motion_matching.mojo#L530) |

For the animator's review, capture the same stop, pivot and recovery from a fixed side view and a three-quarter gameplay view. During the stop, look for the pelvis settling over the support foot. During the pivot, check when the supporting heel releases. During recovery, check that the planted hand carries weight until the body can rise over its feet. Keep the take, frame numbers and contact traces together so an artist can point an engineer to the exact frame.

Epic's Game Animation Sample provides a practical comparison for the actor course. It combines capsule-driven locomotion, motion selection and traversal across several characters, with controls for inspecting movement and pose. Jolt's collision documentation provides a comparison for the physics tests. It describes a full shape cast for translation and documents the limit for rapidly rotating long objects. Both are behavioral references for Flint's own Mojo implementations. [Epic Game Animation Sample](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine) · [Jolt continuous collision detection](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/#continuous-collision-detection)

For a later research milestone, MaskedMimic demonstrates steering a physically simulated humanoid using partial joint targets and movement commands. A bounded Flint experiment would track one authored recovery after a sideways shove. Use the existing motor-driven ragdoll as the baseline and compare recovery success, stance drift, pose error and runtime cost on identical inputs. This gives a future learned controller a concrete reason to earn its place. [MaskedMimic project and paper](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/maskedmimic/)
